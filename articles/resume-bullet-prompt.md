# The Resume Bullet Prompt That Actually Works (With the Real AI Output)

Most resume advice tells you to "use strong action verbs and quantify your impact." That is true, and almost useless, because the moment you sit down to rewrite a bullet like "Responsible for helping customers and handling complaints," you still have to do all the work yourself.

This is the first prompt published by AI Made Simple, a channel built on one rule: every prompt is actually run, and the output is shown unedited. Here is the prompt, what it produced, and how to use it without falling into the one trap that makes AI resumes backfire.

## The prompt

```text
Here is a weak resume bullet from a retail sales associate: "Responsible for helping customers and handling complaints." Rewrite it into 3 strong resume bullets. Use strong action verbs and results language, but do NOT invent numbers — use [brackets] as placeholders where a number would go.
```

Two parts of this prompt do the heavy lifting. First, it asks for three options instead of one rewrite, so you can choose the version closest to your real job. Second — and this is the part most prompts leave out — it forbids invented numbers and demands `[brackets]` wherever a number belongs. AI writing tools love to invent a tidy "increased satisfaction by 20%." If you did not measure it, that number is a lie on your resume, and it tends to surface at the worst possible moment: in the interview, when someone asks how you measured it.

## The real output (unedited)

> 1. Delivered personalized customer service by identifying customer needs, recommending relevant products, and resolving inquiries to enhance customer satisfaction and drive repeat business.
> 2. Resolved customer complaints and escalated complex issues with professionalism and urgency, minimizing service disruptions and strengthening customer loyalty.
> 3. Managed high-volume customer interactions while addressing product questions, processing requests, and resolving concerns, contributing to improved service efficiency and achievement of [sales/service targets].

Notice what changed. "Responsible for" — the weakest possible opening, because it describes a job description rather than a person — is gone. Each bullet now leads with a verb (Delivered, Resolved, Managed), names a skill a hiring manager scans for, and ends with the business reason the task exists. Notice also what did not change: no fake percentages, no invented team sizes. The single bracket, `[sales/service targets]`, marks exactly where your real detail goes.

## How to use it on your own resume

1. Replace the sample bullet in the prompt with one of your own, word for word as it appears on your resume today.
2. Keep the instruction about brackets exactly as written. It is the safety rail.
3. From the three outputs, pick the one that is most true, not the one that sounds most impressive.
4. Replace the bracket with a real detail — a target you actually hit, a queue you actually handled — or delete that phrase if you have no honest version of it.
5. Repeat bullet by bullet. One prompt, one bullet, one honest edit at a time beats pasting your whole resume in and accepting whatever comes back.

## The bigger lesson

The value of an AI prompt for job hunting is not that it writes for you. It is that it shows you the distance between what you did and how a strong candidate would describe the same work. The brackets are the point: they show you precisely which facts about your own experience are worth going and finding.
